#+title: A first model
#+description: A knob, typed from scratch: a 2D profile, its distance field, rounding by blending, a revolve.

This part starts from an empty file and makes a knob that can be printed.
On the way, it shows the preview, the 2D views, completion, and how to change a number without retyping it.

* Open a script

Visit a new file, =knob.keycap=, and press =C-c C-c=.
The preview opens next to it, and says there is nothing in view yet.

The preview is a side window.
From now on, it updates every time you stop typing for a moment, so you don't have to save to see a change.
While a line is half typed, the script has an error, and the preview keeps the last model that worked; [[file:../manual/troubleshooting.html#when-a-script-fails][When a script fails]] in the manual shows what an error looks like.

* A block and two cutters

The knob is a profile, its cross-section, revolved around its axis.
The profile is a block with two cuts: a waist for the fingers, and a dish on top.
Type the dimensions, the block and the cutters:

#+begin_src keycap :model knob :alt The block in white, and the cutters in orange: an ellipse at each side for the waist, and a flat one along the top for the dish
// Knob: a round handle with a dished top and a waist for the fingers.

const D = 60.0;             // handle diameter
const H = 30.0;             // handle height
const ROUNDING = 2.0;

// The knob's cross-section before the cuts.
let block = rect([D, H]).move([0, H / 2]);

// The cutters: the waist on both sides, and the dish.
let waist = ellipse([D / 6, H / 2]).move([D / 2, H / 2]).symmetric_x();
let dish = ellipse([D / 2, H / 12]).move([0, H]);

draw(block);
draw_rgb([waist, dish], 0.95, 0.55, 0.2);
#+end_src

These are 2D shapes, so the preview shows them from above, as a distance field.
Every point is shaded by how far it is from the nearest outline, in bands: in the shape's colour inside, and dark grey outside.
=draw= draws a shape in white, and =draw_rgb= in a colour, its red, green and blue each from 0 to 1.

keycap is built around this.
A shape is a function rather than a list of triangles: for a given point, it says how far away the surface is, negative inside.
The script builds it as an expression of =x= and =y=, a /tree/, which keycap evaluates at every point it shows; [[file:../manual/writing-models.html#fields-and-trees][Fields and trees]] in the manual has more.
Everything else is arithmetic on such functions:

- =move= shifts a shape, and =symmetric_x= reads it at =abs(x)=, so the waist's ellipse on the right is mirrored to the left.
- =min(a, b)= is the nearer of two surfaces, which gives both shapes: a union, the same as =union(a, b)=.

* Cutting

Replace the =draw= lines with:

#+begin_src keycap :model knob :alt The block with the cutters taken away: sharp cusps where each cut meets the block's corner
draw(block.difference(union(waist, dish)));
#+end_src

=difference= takes the cutters away.
With no shape in colour, the field is blue inside and orange outside, and white where the distance is zero, which is the surface itself.

It works, but both ellipses touch the outer corners of the block, and there are sharp cusps where they meet.
A knob shouldn't have those.

* Opening it

In OpenSCAD, a profile like this is rounded with =offset(r) offset(-r)=: shrink it, then grow it back, and every convex corner comes back round.
On a field, the two would just cancel out.
The closest thing is to combine the shapes smoothly in the first place.
Replace the =draw= line with:

#+begin_src keycap :model knob :alt The profile with both cuts' edges rounded and the cusps gone
// The cuts, with every edge they leave rounded.
let profile = block.smooth_difference([waist, dish], 2.0 * ROUNDING);
draw(profile);
#+end_src

- =smooth_difference= takes the cutters away and blends the edges they leave over a width, here twice the rounding radius.
- The cutters are blended into each other first, so the rim between the waist and the dish is rounded too.
- =smooth_union= and =smooth_intersection= blend the other two ways of combining shapes.

=C-c C-d= on =smooth_difference= shows its documentation, with what it does at the cusps and how it compares to OpenSCAD's offsets.

* Into three dimensions

Replace the =draw= line again.
As you type =profile.revo=, completion offers what fits:

#+begin_src keycap :model knob :at "profile.revo|lve" :show completion :alt The profile revolved into a knob, shaded; completion offers revolve and the other names like it
// The profile spun around the Z axis.
let handle = profile.revolve();
draw(handle);
#+end_src

=revolve= spins the profile around the Z axis: its X becomes the radius and its Y the height.
The script is now 3D, so the preview shows it shaded.

* Changing your mind

Put point after the =30= of =H = 30.0= and press =C-c C-n= a few times.
Each press adds one to the digit before point, and the knob grows in the preview; right after a press, =n= and =p= keep nudging without the prefix.

#+begin_src keycap :model knob :at "H = 30|.0" :nudge 3 :alt The knob taller after nudging H; the echo area shows the step
#+end_src

With point after the =.0=, the step is 0.1 instead, and a numeric prefix multiplies it.
This is handy for dimensions you are not sure about: you can try a few values instead of guessing.

* Looking at it

With the preview selected:

- =s=, =z=, =d=, =m= :: shaded, depth, distance and inside-or-outside views.
- =1=, =3=, =7=, =0= :: front, right, top and isometric; in a 2D view, the slice across Y, X or Z.
- =[=, =]= :: move the slice; a numeric prefix puts it at that coordinate.
- arrows, =M-= arrows, =+=, =-= :: turn, pan and zoom; dragging and the wheel do the same.
- =p= :: perspective or orthographic.

The mouse works too: dragging turns the model, the wheel zooms, and with Shift held, the wheel and dragging move the slice through the model.
The words at the start of the header line are buttons for the views, the cameras and the slices; [[file:03-keyboard-and-mouse.org][part 3]] goes through all of it.

Press =d= and then =1= to see the knob cut in half along Y, with its distance field around it.

#+begin_src keycap :model knob :slice y :probe "0 19" :alt A slice through the knob across Y, with the mouse's position and the distance there in the header
#+end_src

When the mouse is over the image, the header line shows the point under it and the distance there.
Slices let you check things you can't see from outside, like wall thickness, holes and clearances.
Holding Shift and dragging up or down scrubs the slice through the whole knob, which is quicker than stepping with =]=.
