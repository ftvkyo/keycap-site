#+title: Exporting and printing
#+description: From the script to an STL a slicer can use: bounds, resolution and tolerance.

A field doesn't have triangles, so to get a mesh, keycap has to make one.
Exporting samples the field on a grid inside the bounds, builds a mesh from it, and simplifies the mesh.

* Exporting

In the script of the knob, press =C-c C-e= (or =e= in the preview).
By default, the file is named after the script, =knob.stl=, and saved next to it.

#+begin_src keycap :model knob :after "02-fasteners.org" :show export :alt The knob exported: the echo area reports the triangles kept, of how many, and within what tolerance
#+end_src

The export runs in the background.
The header shows its progress, and the preview keeps working; =C-c C-k=, or =C-g= in the preview, cancels it.
A cancelled export doesn't touch the file that was there before.

* Bounds

Only what is within the bounds is exported.
Without =bounds(...)=, the preview finds the bounds itself, from the field, and the header says =fitted bounds= and their size.
=bounds([x0, y0, z0], [x1, y1, z1])= in the script sets them explicitly, and warns you when a shape sticks out of them.
=b= in the preview switches between the two.

* Resolution and tolerance

- =resolution(n)= in the script, or =keycap-export-resolution=, is the density of the grid: =n= cells per millimetre.
  Details smaller than a cell can be lost; try a part at 5 (cells of 0.2 mm), then export it at the default 10.
- The mesh is then simplified wherever that changes it by less than =keycap-export-tolerance=, which is a tenth of a cell by default.
  Flat faces become a few large triangles, and curves keep as many as they need.

The echo area says how many triangles the grid made, how many are left, and how close they stay to the surface.
For a twisted vase, 1.5 million triangles become 49,000, and the file is 2.4 MB instead of 74 MB.

* Printing

The STL is closed and watertight, so any slicer can use it.
A few things that help:

- Model in millimetres, with the part standing on z = 0, as slicers expect.
- Use the clearances from the prelude (=clearance=, the =fit= of =screw_hole=) instead of guessing; tune them to your printer once, in one place.
- Before exporting, look at a slice through anything that has to fit: =d=, then =1=, =3= or =7=, and =[=, =]= or Shift and the wheel.
- Print a test piece before relying on a fit, and don't use a printed part where its failing could hurt someone: nothing here has been tested for strength (see [[file:../about.org::#no-warranty][No warranty]]).

The [[file:../manual/index.html][manual]] has the rest, and the [[file:../gallery/index.html][gallery]] has an example for most kinds of parts.
