* Who made it and how it was made

#+begin_human Rei
I'm Rei ([[https://ftvkyo.me][ftvkyo]]).
I design functional parts for 3D printing, and I wanted a better way to write them than OpenSCAD.

keycap is written using [[https://claude.com/claude-code][Claude Code]], an AI coding assistant.
I set the goals and made the design decisions.
Claude Code wrote most of the code, the tests and the manual.
Not every line has been read by a person yet.

Most of the designs in the gallery are mine, and most of their =.keycap= scripts are Claude Code's ports of my OpenSCAD originals.
Every picture of a model on this site is rendered by =keycap= from a script, so the docs should be at least somewhat reliable.

=keycap= does not need AI to run.
It is ordinary Emacs Lisp, Rust and Rhai (Rust-inspired scripting language).
It works offline and is decently readable.
My goal here is using AI to build a tool that does not depend on it.
#+end_human

* Goals and Plans

#+begin_human Rei
I want to use =keycap= to create practical and impractical 2D and 3D designs.
I felt like OpenSCAD had exhausted itself as a hobby, and I often wanted to model things that were awkward or borderline impossible to do using OpenSCAD.

I plan to continue using and expanding =keycap=.
Currently, it works around some of the issues in its dependencies, and I hope to find the time to contribute improvements upstream, where they are welcome.

First and foremost, I hope that this package is useful for my friends.
#+end_human

It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by fidget, Halfspace or libfive, nor by Rhai or tree-sitter-rhai.

- [[https://github.com/mkeeter/fidget][fidget]], by Matt Keeter, evaluates, renders and meshes the implicit surfaces, and does it fast.
  It does most of the actual work.
- [[https://github.com/mkeeter/halfspace][Halfspace]] is Matt Keeter's modelling environment for fidget, and it showed what an editor for fidget could look like.

* No warranty
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: no-warranty
:END:

keycap, its parts library and the designs here come as they are, without warranty of any kind.
The dimensions of standard parts are from tables that makers and distributors publish, not from the standards themselves; they can be wrong or out of date.
Check them against the part you have, and print or cut a test piece before relying on a fit.
Nothing here has been tested for strength: printed threads, heat-set inserts, hinges and joints can fail in ways a model doesn't show.
Don't use a printed or cut part to carry a load, or where its failing could hurt someone.

3D printing and laser cutting have hazards of their own, fumes and fire among them; follow your machine's and your workspace's rules.
Never laser-cut PVC, vinyl or polycarbonate; the manual's [[file:manual/editing-and-previewing.html#exporting-for-laser-cutting][laser cutting]] section says more.
