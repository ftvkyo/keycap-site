keycap is an Emacs IDE for designing parts for 3D printing and laser cutting.
You write a script, and the model it describes is shown next to it, updating as you type.

#+begin_src keycap :open knob.keycap :exports results :alt A knob's script in Emacs on the left, its rendered model on the right
#+end_src

The scripts are written in [[https://rhai.rs][Rhai]] and run by Matt Keeter's [[https://github.com/mkeeter/fidget][fidget]].
In fidget, a shape is a formula: a function of =x=, =y= and =z= that is negative inside the shape, positive outside, and tells how far away the surface is.
Rounded edges, walls of even thickness and fits with a clearance are easy to make this way, and surfaces have no facets however close you zoom in.

* Features
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-it-is-like
:END:

- Two windows: the script on the left, its preview on the right.
- The preview updates as you type.
  It takes about 0.3 s from the last key press to a new frame, and most of that is the pause that waits for you to stop typing.
- Everything can be done from the keyboard; the mouse can also turn and zoom the model.
- Errors are marked on the exact token, together with the call that led there.
- Completion and ElDoc know every shape and function, and the fields of the shape whose map you are typing.
- =C-c C-n= nudges the number at point and the model updates, so you can try a few values for a dimension instead of guessing.
- 2D views slice the model anywhere and show its distance field, e.g. how much clearance there is around a hole.
- =C-c C-e= exports an STL, simplified to a given tolerance.
  For example, a vase that meshes into 1.5 million triangles is exported as 49,000 within 0.04 mm.

* Compared to OpenSCAD
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: why-fields
:END:

Most of the models on this site are ports of my older designs, so the two can be compared directly.
Almost every port was checked against its original's export, by slices or by points on its surface.
Most agree within 0.3 mm, and the larger differences are mostly where a port rounds a corner that its original left sharp or faceted.

| Design         | OpenSCAD                                 | keycap                                       |
|----------------+------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------|
| Twisted vase   | 72 layers of 180 points stitched by hand | 60 lines of maths, smooth at any zoom        |
| DNA loop       | a polyhedron                             | helices that stay round                      |
| Wall thickness | even across each layer                   | even square to the surface, however it leans |

Some things are worse, though.
Meshes are made on a grid, so some shapes take longer to export.
There is also no =hull()= and no STL import.
The [[file:manual/coming-from-openscad.html#porting-from-openscad][porting table]] in the manual lists what translates and what doesn't.

* The parts

- keycap :: the major mode, the preview and the export, in Emacs Lisp.
- rhai-ts-mode :: a general Emacs mode for Rhai on tree-sitter; it can be used on its own.
- keycap-server :: a small Rust server that evaluates, renders and meshes scripts with fidget, on the GPU if there is one and on all CPU cores otherwise.
  It also draws text and imports SVG.
- The prelude :: a library of shapes written in Rhai: rounded and chamfered combinations, fasteners with print clearances, gears, helices, Bézier strokes, easing and lofts.
  Its modules add screws, nuts, threads, heat-set inserts, bearings, magnets, aluminium extrusions and laser-cut joints.
- The manual :: all of the above in one document, as Info in Emacs and as [[file:manual/index.html][web pages]] here, with a picture of every shape.
