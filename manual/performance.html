fidget evaluates the tree over boxes of space with interval arithmetic and skips the boxes it proves empty or full, so speed depends on the tree’s size and on how well intervals see through it.

  • Copies cost: grid_array(step, [10, 10]) is a hundred copies in the tree; repeat_grid is one.
  • polygon costs about 40 operations per point and rounded_polygon about 90 per rounded corner, and intervals see through neither well: a prism on a 64-gon of points renders in about 5 CPU-seconds, one on regular_polygon(64, r) in 0.7. A single convex loop of straight edges takes a path of about 15 operations a corner, with the same values to rounding and intervals that see its inside, and its prism renders in 0.6; #{convex: false} keeps the general one. Draw with primitives, planes and their combinations where they fit, and keep points for outlines measured or traced.
  • keycap/points’s combinations work when the script runs, in about n * m steps for outlines of n and m edges: scraper.keycap’s six take a tenth of a second.
  • text and svg cost about 45 operations per edge of their outlines, but boxes around their contours let a render skip the ones far from each region: a line of ten letters is about 14,000 operations and renders in a tenth of a second. An SVG with thousands of small curves, such as a KiCad board’s drill holes, is hundreds of thousands, and takes about a second to load and as long to render on the CPU; import it alone, or the part of it you need with id. The libfive font is a fortieth of the size of a real one.
  • Exact fields and plain min and max prune well; %, floor and the selection tricks of Choices without if prune poorly, and so render slower.
  • So do sin, cos and atan2 of a coordinate, sind and the other degree functions too: the GPU sees no region near them empty or full, and the CPU calls a library function for every point. The prelude’s eases, turns and shapes about the Z axis use polynomials and atan instead, which prune.
  • Mirrors fold space cheaper than angles do: abs(x) and reflections across lines cost a few operations, repeat_polar about a hundred for its angle, sine and cosine; for shapes symmetric about their axis, fold by mirrors, as vase-koch.keycap does.
  • normalize_z, and so loft, evaluates the shape twice, as loft_tilted does its profile, and a shape given at two slices of a loft is evaluated twice more.
  • A rounding or a chamfer whose size varies over space evaluates its shapes twice, to keep the field a distance.
  • A folded shape hides its extent from interval arithmetic, and so does a turning one, except about the Z axis. max with a cylinder, box or torus that contains it changes no value, but lets fitted bounds find it and renders skip the space around it.
  • bezier_stroke costs about 150 operations per piece of curve, but boxes around the pieces let a render skip the ones far from each region: feather.keycap’s two hundred barbs load in three seconds and render in about one.
  • A large, loose bounding region wastes work; set bounds when the fitted region is too large, or when an unbounded axis leaves it guessing.
  • The preview renders where it is sooner, on the GPU or the CPU; G overrides it. The GPU renders a slow frame in tiles, each well short of the time after which the driver resets the GPU, and shorter still while an edit or a drag makes frames that the next replaces.
  • Export time grows with the surface area times the resolution squared: try a part at 5 cells per mm, then export it at the default 10, or at 20 for fine details. The memory does too, which makes a long thin part, such as feather.keycap, cheap at a resolution that a vase of the same size could not afford.
  • A script stops after 20 million operations, which only an endless loop reaches.
