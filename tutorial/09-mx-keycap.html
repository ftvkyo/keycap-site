An MX-style keycap

Part 9 of 9

Drafted with AI, not yet reviewed

This part builds an MX-style keycap from an empty file. It is a port of mx_v4.scad: an almost square base that eases into a rounder, tilted pad with a dish, sculpted differently for each row of the keyboard.

This part is longer than the others, and the side of the cap takes some maths. It can be typed without following every line; each step explains the idea and what to look at in the preview. The finished script makes the same cap as the original, its outside within a few hundredths of a millimetre.

Two squircles

Visit a new file, mx.keycap, and press C-c C-c. 

// An MX keycap: a squircle base easing into a tilted pad with a dish.

// --- Outlines ---

// The numbers the cap's functions need: a function sees only its arguments.
let o = #{
    base: 18.0,                 // side at z = 0
    base_squareness: 0.99,      // 0 a circle, towards 1 a square
    pad: 13.0,                  // side of the top
    pad_squareness: 0.5,
};

draw(squircle(o.base / 2.0, o.base_squareness)
    .difference(squircle(o.pad / 2.0, o.pad_squareness)));

The base of the cap, at the bottom, is an 18 mm squircle that is almost a square; the pad at the top is a 13 mm one that is much rounder. squircle(r, s) from the prelude is the original’s f_squircle. r is half the side, and the squareness s goes from a circle at 0 towards a square at 1. The draw takes the pad out of the base, so the distance view shows both outlines at once, as white lines. Seen from above, the side of the cap will fill the ring between them.

A function in a script sees only its arguments. So the numbers that the cap’s functions need go into one map, o, which each of them gets; the map grows as the script does.

The pad’s plane

Each row of the keyboard gets its own pad: higher or lower, and tilted towards the typist or away. In v4, a row is three numbers: the height of the pad’s middle, its tilt about Y, and how far along X its middle sits. The rows run along Y, and the typist sits at +X, so the top row’s pad leans towards them and the bottom rows’ away. 

// --- Row ---

// Each row's pad: the height of its middle, its tilt about Y in degrees,
// and how far along X its middle sits.
const ROWS = [
    #{height: 12.5, angle: 10.0, offset: -1.0},
    #{height: 9.5, angle: 7.5, offset: -1.0},
    #{height: 7.5, angle: 3.0, offset: -0.5},
    #{height: 7.5, angle: -3.0, offset: 0.0},
    #{height: 8.5, angle: -7.5, offset: -0.5},
    #{height: 8.5, angle: -7.5, offset: -1.0},
    #{height: 7.0, angle: 7.5, offset: 0.0},
];
const ROW = 1;                  // 1 top, 4 home, 6 bottom, 7 thumb

o += ROWS[ROW - 1];
o.dish_depth = 0.75;            // from the rim down to the dish's middle

/// Moves a shape from the origin to `dz` above the pad's middle,
/// tilted with the pad.
fn on_pad(shape, o, dz) {
    shape.rotate_y(o.angle).move([o.offset, 0.0, o.height + dz])
}

draw(union(
    squircle(o.base / 2.0, o.base_squareness).extrude(0.4),
    on_pad(squircle(o.pad / 2.0, o.pad_squareness).extrude(0.4),
           o, o.dish_depth / 2.0)
));
  • o += ROWS[ROW - 1] adds the row’s three numbers to the map.
  • on_pad moves a shape onto the pad’s plane: turned by the row’s tilt, and moved to dz above the pad’s middle. The side, the dish, the legend and the dot all use this plane.
  • The rim, the top edge of the side, is half the dish’s depth above the pad’s middle, and the bottom of the dish is as far below it.
  • The two outlines are drawn as thin plates, the base at z = 0 and the pad on the rim.

Press d for the distance view and 1 to slice across Y, as above: the pad’s profile tilts down towards +X, and sits 1 mm towards -X. Put point after the 1 of ROW = 1 and step through the rows with C-c C-n, then n and p. Past row 7 there is no row, and the header says so. Go back to row 1 before the next step.

The side

v4 builds the side from slices. It computes about a hundred outlines as lists of points, from the base up to the pad, each one a bit smaller, rounder and more tilted than the one below, and stitches them into a polyhedron. loft_tilted from the prelude makes the same solid as a field. It takes the outline as a 2D shape that changes from slice to slice, and works out which slice a point is on. 

// --- Side ---

/// The side, from the base at z = 0 up to the rim,
/// where the pad's plane cuts it.
fn side(o) {
    // The slice's outline: the base's and the pad's radii, blended.
    let s = clamp(z, 0.0, 1.0);
    let size = lerp(o.pad, o.base, acos(s) * 2.0 / PI) * 0.5;
    let pad = squircle_ray(x, y, o.pad_squareness);
    let base = squircle_ray(x, y, o.base_squareness);
    let t = square(square(s));
    let r = size * lerp(base.r, pad.r, t);
    let dr = size * lerp(base.r * base.slope, pad.r * pad.slope, t);
    let outline = polar_outline(r, dr);
    let rim = [o.offset, 0.0, o.height + o.dish_depth * 0.5];
    outline.loft_tilted(rim, o.angle)
}

draw(side(o).intersection(-z));
  • Inside the outline, x and y are coordinates within the slice, and z is the slice itself: 0 at the base, 1 at the rim. loft_tilted also looks a little past both ends, where acos has no value, so s is z clamped to them.
  • squircle_ray(x, y, s) is the squircle as its radius in the direction of a point, and how fast that radius turns. v4 blends the base’s points into the pad’s along each ray from the middle, and blending radii does the same. polar_outline makes a field from a radius and its slope.
  • size eases from the base’s half side down to the pad’s along an arc sine, whose infinite slope at the top rounds the shoulder into the pad.
  • The shape blends from the base’s squircle to the pad’s by s to the fourth power, so the side stays square most of the way up.
  • loft_tilted puts the slice z on a plane through the point z of the way from the middle of the base to the middle of the rim, tilted by z of the row’s tilt, and cuts the top on the rim’s plane. (v4 turns its slices by an angle that grows evenly; here the angle’s tangent grows evenly, which differs by under a hundredth of a millimetre.)

Its field is a distance square to the wall, also where the wall leans. This matters for the shell, in a later step. intersection(-z) cuts the side off at z = 0.

Press d and 1 for the slice across Y through the middle. The bands inside are evenly spaced along the walls, the leaning one at +X too.

The dish

v4’s dish is more slices: from a flat middle up to the rim, each one the pad’s outline, rising along half a cosine. As a field, it is a height: how far a point is above the dish’s surface. 

// --- Dish ---

o.dish_flat = 1.0;              // radius of the flat in the dish's middle

fn dish(o) {
    // The dish's frame: w up from the pad's plane, u along it, sheared.
    let tilt = o.angle * PI / 180.0;
    let qx = x - o.offset;
    let qz = z - (o.height - o.dish_depth * 0.5);
    let w = qx * sin(tilt) + qz * cos(tilt);
    let u = qx * cos(tilt) - qz * sin(tilt) + w * tan(tilt);

    // A half cosine from the flat middle up to the rim, divided by its slope.
    let pad = squircle_ray(u, y, o.pad_squareness);
    let span = o.pad * 0.5 - o.dish_flat;
    let k = clamp((hypot(u, y) / pad.r - o.dish_flat) / span, 0.0, 1.0);
    let depth = o.dish_depth * cos(tilt);
    let rise = depth * (1.0 - cos(PI * k)) * 0.5;
    let slope = depth * PI * 0.5 / span * sin(PI * k)
        * sqrt(1.0 + square(pad.slope)) / pad.r;
    (w - rise) / sqrt(1.0 + square(slope))
}

draw(max(side(o), dish(o)).intersection(-z));
  • w is the height above the plane through the dish’s middle, square to it, and u the position along it. v4 lifts each of the dish’s slices straight up, along Z, while the plane they lie in is tilted; in the tilted frame, that lift is a shear, and w * tan(tilt) undoes it.
  • k is how far out the point is, from the edge of the flat middle (0) to the rim (1), measured along the pad’s squircle.
  • rise is half a cosine of k, so the dish is flat in the middle and at the rim.
  • Dividing by the rise’s slope keeps the field a distance across the dish, for the shell under it.

max(side(o), dish(o)) keeps what is both inside the side and under the dish.

An even shell

The cap is hollow, with a 1 mm wall. v1 to v3 made the inside by scaling the body down, which keeps 1 mm only at the base: the wall thins as it rises, and the top is 1 to 2 mm thick. v4 has a second, inner polyhedron, made by hand, and its walls are 1.0 to 1.3 mm, and 1 to 2 mm under the dish. On a field, shell keeps a wall of a given thickness inside the surface, measured square to it: 

// --- Shell ---

const WALL = 1.0;

let outer = max(side(o), dish(o));
let body = outer.intersection(-z);
let shell = outer.shell(WALL).intersection(-z);
draw(shell);
  • outer is the side and the dish together, and body is that cut off at z = 0. body isn’t drawn; the stem and the ribs will be cut to it.
  • outer.shell(WALL) is the wall, and intersection(-z) cuts it at z = 0. Below z = 0, loft_tilted carries the side straight down, so the inside reaches through the bottom, and the cut leaves it open.

The slice across Y shows the wall as the band between two white lines, the same width all round, under the dish too. Use ] and [ to move the slice, or drag with Shift held to scrub through the cap, and 3 to slice across X. On most of the cap, the wall is 1 mm to within a few hundredths. It is thinnest, about 0.94 mm, in the base’s tight corners, where the squircle’s field is least exact. Elsewhere, and across Z everywhere, a slice can cut the sloping sides slantwise, and the band is wider than the wall: tilt the slice with {, }, M-{ and M-}, 5° a press, or C-drag, until the band is narrowest, and it cuts the wall square.

The stem

The stem is what fits onto the switch: a round post with a cross-shaped hole. 

// --- Stem ---

const STEM_RADIUS = 2.5;
const STEM_LIFT = 1.0;          // the stem's foot above z = 0
const CROSS = [1.4, 4.1];       // the cross's slot width and arm length
const CROSS_DEPTH = 3.5;

let top = o.height + o.pad;     // above the whole cap
let cross = union(rect([CROSS[0], CROSS[1]]), rect([CROSS[1], CROSS[0]]));
let stem = circle(STEM_RADIUS).extrude([STEM_LIFT, top])
    .difference(cross.extrude([STEM_LIFT - 1.0, STEM_LIFT + CROSS_DEPTH]));
draw(union(shell, stem.intersection(body)));
  • The post goes from 1 mm above the bottom edge up to top, which is above the whole cap, and intersection(body) cuts it off at the cap’s outer surface; the shell covers the rest. Everything inside the cap is made this way: longer than it needs to be, then cut.
  • The cross is two rectangles, 1.4 by 4.1 mm. These are the clearances: the switch’s own cross is a little smaller.
  • The cross is cut 3.5 mm deep, starting 1 mm below the post’s foot, so that it is open at the bottom.

To check the fit, press d, 7 for a slice across Z, and M-3 ] to put it at z = 3. With the mouse on the middle line of an arm of the cross, the header shows about 0.7: the field of the cross is exact, so this is half the slot’s width.

In the shaded view, turn the cap over with the arrow keys to see the stem from below.

Supports and row markers

// --- Supports ---

const STEM_HEIGHT = 4.5;        // the stem's free length, below the ribs
const SUPPORT = 1.0;            // the ribs' thickness

let ribs = union(rect([SUPPORT, o.base]), rect([o.base, SUPPORT]))
    .extrude([STEM_LIFT + STEM_HEIGHT, top]);

// Posts inside the -X wall, one per row number.
let spread = o.base - WALL * 6.0;
let posts = [];
for i in 0..ROW {
    posts.push(circle(0.75)
        .move([-o.base / 2.0 + WALL, -spread / 2.0 + spread / 6.0 * i]));
}
let markers = union(posts).extrude([0.0, top]);

let cap = union(shell, union([stem, ribs, markers]).intersection(body));
draw(cap);
  • ribs are two thin walls in a cross, from the top of the stem’s free length up into the top of the cap, so the stem doesn’t wobble. Like the stem, they are cut to body.
  • markers are posts inside the -X wall, one per row number, to tell the rows apart from below: a row 1 cap has one, a row 6 cap six.
  • cap is the shell and everything inside it.

Press d and 1. The slice across Y through the middle now runs along a rib, so it is solid from the top down to the stem; M-3 ] moves it off the rib.

A legend

v4’s caps are blank, so this step is new. The letter is cut into the dish, following its curve, the same depth everywhere. 

// --- Legend ---

const LEGEND = "Q";
const LEGEND_DEPTH = 0.4;

let letters = text(LEGEND, #{size: 8.0, halign: "center", valign: "center"})
    .rotate_z(90.0)
    .extrude([-1.0, 2.0]);
let engraved = cap.engrave(on_pad(letters, o, -o.dish_depth / 2.0),
                           -dish(o), LEGEND_DEPTH);
draw(engraved);
  • text makes the letter, centred, at size 8, in Liberation Sans unless the options name a font (part 6). rotate_z(90.0) turns it to read from +X, the typist’s side, and extrude([-1.0, 2.0]) makes it a solid that reaches through the dish.
  • on_pad puts it in the middle of the dish, tilted with it.
  • engrave cuts it in, but only within LEGEND_DEPTH of -dish(o), the space above the dish. So the floor of the letter follows the dish’s curve, 0.4 mm under it everywhere.

Press s and 3 to look from +X, then turn the camera up with the arrow keys: the Q reads upright. In the slice across Y, the cuts are the same depth on both sides of the dish.

A homing dot

The home row’s index keys, F and J, usually have a bump to find them by touch. v4 sinks half of a flattened ellipsoid into the middle of the dish; a legend there would run into it, so a key gets one or the other. 

// --- Homing dot ---

const DOT = true;               // a bump in the dish's middle, not the legend

let dot = on_pad(ellipsoid([0.75, 0.75, 1.0 / 3.0]), o, -o.dish_depth / 2.0);
let key = if DOT { union(cap, dot) } else { engraved };
draw(key);

DOT picks the dot or the legend. For a real homing key, set ROW to 4 as well.

Exporting

// --- Export ---

bounds([-10.0, -10.0, -0.5], [10.0, 10.0, 14.5]);
resolution(20);
draw(key);
  • bounds is a box around the cap: 20 mm square, from just below z = 0 to above the top of row 1, the tallest.
  • resolution(20) is 20 cells per millimetre: twice the default 10 that part 8 exports at, because the cross has to fit the switch to a few hundredths.

Press C-c C-e, and mx.stl appears next to the script. It takes a few seconds, and the echo area says how many triangles the mesh has, before and after simplifying.

What to change

The design lives in a few numbers, and these are the ones to nudge with C-c C-n:

  • ROW, for the other rows’ caps
  • in o, pad, pad_squareness and base_squareness for the shape, and dish_depth and dish_flat for the dish
  • WALL, for a thicker shell
  • CROSS, if the cap is too tight or too loose on the switch
    • with point after the last digit of 1.4, C-c C-n steps it by 0.1; after 1.40, by 0.01
  • LEGEND, its size, font and depth

v4 also has 2u caps with stabiliser stems, and a solid block around the cross for switches that aren’t box switches. This part leaves both out to stay shorter.
