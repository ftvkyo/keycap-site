Keyboard and mouse

Part 3 of 9

Drafted with AI, not yet reviewed

Everything in keycap has a key, and most things can be done with the mouse too. This part takes the knob from the last two parts and goes through both ways of working, then lists every action with its key and its gesture. Nothing new gets modelled here.

Two ways to work

With the keyboard alone, the script’s window stays selected while you type, and the preview follows on its own. The preview’s commands that are needed from the script have C-c keys: C-c C-c shows it, C-c C-v switches its view. For the rest, C-x o moves to the preview, where single keys turn the model, switch views and move the slice, and C-x o moves back. There, ? lists the most used keys in the echo area, and in a 2D view, the slice’s keys. The preview also shows the list by itself, the first time in a session that it shows a 3D view, and the first time it shows a 2D one.

With a hand on the mouse, the keyboard stays in the script and the mouse works on the preview. Dragging, the wheel and the header line’s buttons leave the keyboard where it is, so you can keep typing while the model turns. A click on the preview selects it, for when you want its keys after all.

The header line

The words at the start of the preview’s header line say what it shows, and each of them is a button. Clicking iso opens the cameras:

the view, shaded
the four views, with their keys.
the camera, iso
front, right, top and isometric, perspective, and a reset; once the model is turned by hand, the word is its angles, e.g. -100° 42°.
in a 2D view, the axis and the offset, e.g. y = 15
the axis to slice across; a click on the offset asks for a coordinate, and the wheel over it moves the slice. A tilted slice shows its angles too, e.g. z ⊥ (30°, 0°) at 15, and a click on them levels it.
the bounds and the backend
switch between the script’s bounds and fitted ones, and between the GPU and the CPU.
an error or an export
a click on an error goes to its line in the script, and one on Exporting cancels it.

Hovering a button highlights it and shows a tooltip with what a click does.

The menus

Right-click the preview for its menu: the views, the cameras (or, in 2D, the slices), the toggles and the exports, each with its key.

On the image, the menu starts with “Go to Shape”: it moves to the script, at the draw of the shape under the mouse. Double-clicking a shape does the same, which helps when a script draws several shapes and you want to know which line made which.

The same menu is in the menu bar as Preview, and the script has its own, Keycap, with the script’s commands and their keys. With the menu bar hidden, F10 opens it. With context-menu-mode on, right-clicking a name in the script offers its documentation, as C-c C-d does.

Scrubbing a slice

In a 2D view, holding Shift turns the wheel into a slice control: S-wheel moves the slice a step, the same step as [ and ], and M-wheel moves it ten. S-drag scrubs through the model: dragging up moves the slice forward, and the window’s height covers the whole of the bounds. The frames are rendered at half resolution while you drag, and the header line shows where the slice is.

Click the view’s word, pick “SDF”, then click the axis letter for the slices:

Pick “Across Z”, then scrub up from the bottom: the nut’s hexagon, then the round hole for the bolt, then the knob narrowing at its waist and the ring of bumps in the dish. It is the quickest way to find the thinnest wall in a part.

Tilting a slice

A slice cuts a sloping wall slantwise, and the wall looks thicker on it than it is. Tilt the slice until it cuts the wall square: } and { turn it 5° about the image’s right, M-} and M-{ about its up, and C-drag turns it as if you held it, dragging down bringing its top towards you. A numeric prefix sets the angle, M-3 M-0 } for 30°.

The slice turns about the middle of the bounds, keeps its scale, and the offset runs along its tilted normal; the point under the mouse is read in the model’s own coordinates. r levels it where it is, 1, 3 and 7 slice level across Y, X and Z, and 0 slices through the middle facing the isometric camera, as 0 looks from it in 3D.

Scrubbing a number

The same gestures work on numbers in the script. With the mouse over a digit, S-wheel adds or subtracts one step of that digit, and the preview follows; over the 2 of 12 the step is 1, over the 1 it is 10. S-drag from a digit changes it by a step per column the mouse moves, right to add and left to subtract, and the whole drag is undone with one C-/.

Away from a number, the shifted wheel scrolls sideways and the shifted click opens the font menu, as they do in any buffer.
