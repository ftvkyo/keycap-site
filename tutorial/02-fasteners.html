Bumps and a captive nut

Part 2 of 9

Drafted with AI, not yet reviewed

The knob from the first part still needs a grip, and a way to fix it to something. The prelude has shapes for both, with clearances for printing that are a starting point: print a test, and adjust them for your printer.

Bumps, a nut, a bolt

This is the whole step, in place of the draw line. The preview is still in the section view from the last part; press s to go back to the shaded view. 

// Bumps half sunk into the dish, centred on its surface.
const BUMPS = 12;
const BUMP = D / 12;        // bump diameter
const BUMP_RING = D * 7 / 12;

let bump_z = H - H / 12 * sin(acos(BUMP_RING / D));
let bump = ellipsoid([BUMP / 2, BUMP / 2, BUMP / 4])
    .move([BUMP_RING / 2, 0, bump_z]);
let bumps = bump.polar_array(BUMPS);

// A captive nut from below, so the knob screws onto a bolt.
const BOLT = "M5";
let nut = nut_trap(BOLT, 5).reflect_z();
let bolt = screw_hole(BOLT, 20, #{head: "none"}).reflect_z();
draw(union(handle, bumps).difference(union(nut, bolt)));
  • ellipsoid is a squashed sphere. It is centred on the surface of the dish, so half of it shows, and polar_array(BUMPS) places twelve of them around the Z axis. sin and acos work on plain numbers as well as on shapes; here they find the height of the dish at the ring.
  • nut_trap("M5", 5) is a cutter: a hexagonal pocket for an M5 nut, 5 mm deep, sized from ISO tables with 0.4 mm of clearance. reflect_z flips it so it opens from below.
  • screw_hole("M5", 20, #{head: "none"}) is a 20 mm deep clearance hole for the bolt; reflected, it goes up from the bottom.
  • union(handle, bumps).difference(...) adds the bumps and takes the cutters away.

Push a nut into the pocket, and the knob screws onto an M5 bolt or threaded rod.

Asking for help

With point in a call, the echo area shows the arguments it takes, the one at point highlighted, as it does while you type. Put point after screw_hole(BOLT,:

Options go in a map, #{name: value}, and they are all optional; ElDoc and completion know their names.

C-c C-d on a name shows its documentation: what it makes, where its origin is, all of its options, and an example. With a prefix, or when point is not on a name, it shows the whole reference, grouped like a table of contents.

The manual has the same documentation, for nut_trap, screw_hole and the other fasteners. An error is marked where it happens; When a script fails in the manual shows how.

Seeing inside

The cutters are inside the part, so look at a slice: d for the distance view, then 1 for the slice across Y, through the middle.

The distance bands show how much material is left around the hole and above it, before anything is printed. To see the hexagon of the pocket, press 7 for the slice across Z and M-2 ] to put it 2 mm above the bottom; holding Shift and turning the wheel over the preview moves it too.
